Process

How a project runs.

  1. Conversation

    Twenty minutes. You describe what should happen in the end. I say whether and how I can solve it — and what it costs.

  2. Concept

    One document: idea, visual language, schedule, shoot or generation, delivery dates. Production starts only after that.

  3. Production

    Shoot day, generative production or both. I bring camera, light and sound; models, location and styling coordinated on request.

  4. Post

    Edit, colour, sound, retouch, generative extensions. Two revision rounds are included.

  5. Delivery

    Finished files in every required format and aspect ratio, with subtitles, ready to publish.

FAQ

Briefly answered.

Method and craft

Do you film for real or generate everything?

Both, in whatever ratio the project needs. People, products, rooms — everything where trust is built — I record with the camera. Environments, camera moves and image variants that would be too expensive or impossible to shoot are generated. Whatever was generated is labelled.

How do I recognise what is AI?

By a visible label directly on the asset. Article 50 of the EU AI Act has required this since 2 August 2026 for photorealistic AI-generated content — I comply, not least because it protects the credibility of your campaign.

Can you rebuild a look I saw on social media?

The style, yes. The exact result often depends on the equipment, crew and shooting time behind the original — what fits your budget is something we settle at briefing, not at delivery. This is exactly where the generative layer helps: environments that would need a travel budget are built on the computer.

Why does sound matter so much?

Because bad sound costs more to repair in the edit than to record properly on set — and because viewers forgive a soft image sooner than a voice they cannot understand. Microphone and sound monitoring are part of the shoot day, not of the fixing afterwards.

Price and time

What does a film actually cost?

The ranges above show the order of magnitude. The real price is set by shooting time, crew size, the number of final versions and the usage rights agreed. You receive a written fixed-price quote before production starts.

Why does a thirty-second film cost nearly as much as a long one?

Because length barely determines how much work is involved. Concept, travel, lighting setup, shooting, reviewing footage, edit, colour and sound apply to thirty seconds almost the same as to three minutes. What really moves the price is shooting time, crew size, the number of versions and the usage rights.

How long does a project take from enquiry to file?

A social package is usually delivered within a week of the shoot. Brand and campaign films normally take two to four weeks from first enquiry to handover. The schedule includes a buffer for editing and technical issues — I would rather decline a delivery date without a buffer than miss it later.

What happens if the weather does not cooperate?

For exterior work I plan a fallback date or an interior section from the start; we decide together the day before. Up to which point a postponement is free of charge is stated in the quote — so nobody has to negotiate when it actually happens.

Working together

How many rounds of revisions are included?

Two. You see a first cut, say what should change, and receive the revised version — then once more. Further changes are possible and charged by effort. That limit mainly protects the deadline: without it an edit can be reworked indefinitely and delivery slips.

Who should make the decisions on your side?

Ideally one person with the final word. If several people have a say, it helps to consolidate the feedback first — otherwise contradictory change requests meet in the edit, use up the revision rounds and do not make the film better.

Do I get the film in several formats?

Yes — which versions are included is stated in the quote. Each additional version (9:16, 1:1, 16:9, with and without subtitles) is a separate edit, not an export button: framing, text placement and rhythm have to work again in each format. That is why the number is agreed in advance rather than estimated afterwards.

How does payment work?

A deposit confirms the date and starts production; the balance is due after acceptance. This is standard in the industry and protects both sides: you know from when the shoot day is firmly reserved, and I do not book the same day twice.

Rights and licences

Who owns the finished footage?

The agreed usage rights transfer to you after full payment. Scope, channels, territory and duration are stated explicitly in the quote so that no questions remain open later.

Where does the music come from?

From licensed libraries or from composers, with a licence for the agreed purpose. Music from social media or streaming services cannot simply be laid under a film — for commercial use that gets expensive quickly. Which licence your film carries is part of the handover.

Do I have to arrange models or a location?

Not necessarily — I can organise both. Where people are recognisable, I obtain written consent (model release) before publication.
